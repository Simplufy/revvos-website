Integration

Tekmetric already tells you what happened. The question is what to do next.

Tekmetric is a strong system of record with real reporting: ARO, gross profit per hour, technician efficiency, multi-shop KPIs. None of that is the problem. The problem is that a metric is a rear-view mirror. Knowing yesterday's ARO does not tell a service advisor which three calls to make this morning.

Turn a metric into an action -> All integrations

"Another AI thing bolted onto my shop system?" Fair.

Shop operators are rightly wary of AI added for its own sake, and Tekmetric's own users have said so in public forums. The test we hold ourselves to is simple: does this remove a chore a real person was going to do, or does it just add a surface? If a prepared action does not save an advisor a genuine task, it does not belong in the queue. RevvOS is the layer above Tekmetric that reads what the system already knows and prepares the next step, and it is judged by whether the queue is worth opening, not by whether it uses the word AI.

Automation Inside vs Automation Around

Tekmetric already automates. The question is around what.

Tekmetric runs rule-based triggers and automated status texts on its own data, inside its own interface. That is native workflow automation, and it is the right design for a system of record. An operating layer is a different kind of automation: it runs around the system, across sources the system does not hold.

Native workflow automation

Fires on a condition inside the platform: an RO reaches a status, an inspection is completed, a reminder is due. Reliable, scoped to Tekmetric, driven by Tekmetric's data.

Trigger lives inside one system
Action lands inside the same interface
Blind to the phone, the ad spend, and the reviews
Cross-system automation

Fires on a condition that only exists by combining sources: a missed call with a customer whose last visit declined work, an estimate state next to an ad that drove the lead.

Trigger assembled across systems
Action prepared for a person, then written back
Sees the phone, the spend, and the reviews too

Both are useful. The mistake is expecting a rule inside one system to catch an exception that spans three. That is why the two coexist rather than replace each other.

How An Integration Actually Works

"AI integration" should be a plain sentence, not a slogan.

An integration means three concrete things: what RevvOS reads from Tekmetric, what it writes back, and how often. Read means the repair order, its line items and states, the customer, and the declined lines. Write back means the prepared action and its outcome, so the record stays current without double entry. The frequency and the exact fields depend on Tekmetric's current interface and how your shop is configured.

We will not tell you a specific field syncs until we have confirmed it against your setup, and we will not describe a capability in a way that outruns what the connection actually does. If that sounds like a low bar, it is one a surprising amount of AI marketing fails.

Boundaries

Tekmetric stays the record. RevvOS stays the queue.

RevvOS is not a replacement for Tekmetric and does not try to re-report what Tekmetric already reports well. It reads from Tekmetric, reasons about the exceptions worth acting on, prepares the next step, and writes back. The RO stays the RO. Multi-shop KPIs stay in Tekmetric. What changes is that the number now comes with a short list of things to do about it.

The exact connection depends on Tekmetric's current interfaces and your configuration, which we confirm against your setup.

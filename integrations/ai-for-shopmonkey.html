Two Pitches, One Shop

You will hear both of these this year.

One asks you to move. One asks you to add. They sound similar and they are not, because only one of them puts your shop's history at risk.

Pitch A

"Replace your shop system with our AI platform."

One vendor, one login, the AI and the record in the same place. The appeal is real.

Requires a data migration and a re-training of every advisor and tech
Your repair history, DVI photos, and declined-work record travel with it, or they do not
The board, the parts matrix, and the DVI you already trust get rebuilt from scratch
The AI is only as good as the new record it is standing on
Pitch B

"Keep your shop system. Add the layer on top."

The board and the record stay exactly where they are. Only the exception work changes.

No migration, no re-training, no history at risk
Shopmonkey keeps the board, the DVI, the parts, and the payments
The layer reads the RO and writes back, so the advisor stays in one place
The trade-off is a dependency: the layer is only as good as the connection

We are pitching Pitch B, and we will say the quiet part: it has its own risk, the integration. A layer is worth it only if the connection is real and maintained, which is the first thing we confirm with you.

Automation Inside vs Automation Around

Shopmonkey automates on its board. The layer automates around it.

Shopmonkey runs rule-based automation on its own data: status-triggered texts, email campaigns, workflow nudges. That is native workflow automation, and it belongs inside the system. What it does not do is fire on a condition that only exists by combining the board with the phone, the ad spend, and the customer's history. That cross-system trigger is what "automation around the board" means, and it is the layer's job.

On the integration itself, the plain-sentence version: RevvOS reads Shopmonkey's repair orders, line items and states, and customer records; it writes back the prepared action and its outcome; the frequency and exact fields depend on Shopmonkey's current API and your configuration. We confirm that against your setup rather than describing a sync we have not verified. Nothing is double-entered, and the advisor keeps working in Shopmonkey.

Where The Layer Lives

A board shows the jobs you have. It does not chase the jobs you are losing.

Every shop management system, Shopmonkey included, is organized around the work that is in front of it: the vehicles on the board, the orders in the queue. That is exactly right for a system of record. But the revenue a shop loses rarely sits on the board. It sits in a missed call that never became a job, an estimate that was sent and never answered, and a declined line nobody reopened.

RevvOS is the layer that watches those off-board events: the missed call, the quiet estimate, the declined line. It reads from Shopmonkey, writes back to it, and keeps the advisor in one interface. The board stays the board.

The exact connection depends on Shopmonkey's current API and your configuration, which we confirm against your setup rather than describing a promise.

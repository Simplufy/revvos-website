The glass is installed in an hour. The money can take a month.

GlassBiller runs the glass shop end to end, and it leans hard into the part that hurts: payments and receivables, with insurance and customer money tracked in one place. So the question is not whether the shop can get paid. It is which claims, deductibles, and balances quietly fall out of the follow-up before anyone notices.

One Job, Five Money States

The cash gap is in the middle.

Every glass job moves through the same five money states. The work is done at state one. The money usually lands at state four or five. Everything between is where margin and patience go to die.

01
Installed
The work is finished. Value delivered.
02
Invoiced
Claim filed, deductible split, balance set.
03 · THE GAP
Adjudicating
Waiting on the insurer. Weeks can pass here.
04
Insurer paid
Maybe short. Maybe with a deduction.
05
Balance collected
Deductible and cash jobs close the job.
What GlassBiller carries
The job, the techs, the parts matched by VIN, the routes, the invoices, the receivables tracking, and the QuickBooks sync that keeps the books current.
What the layer watches
The gap itself: which claim has sat too long at state three, which insurer paid short, which deductible never came in, and which customer was never told what they owe.

Four Exceptions That Cost Real Money

The money leaks in four known places.

None of these is a mystery. They are just unbudgeted-for work, which is why they get skipped on a busy Tuesday.

The claim that aged silently

Filed, then nothing. No one sets a clock on state three, so it ages until someone happens to look. The layer flags it at an age you choose and drafts the status check.

The short-paid claim

The insurer paid less than billed. Some shortfalls are legitimate, some are worth a supplement or an appeal. Either way, someone has to decide, and the layer puts the decision in front of a person.

The deductible no one chased

The customer portion is small enough to forget and large enough to add up. The layer keeps it as an open balance with a prepared reminder, so collection is a review, not a research project.

The customer who was never told

A bill arrives for an amount the customer did not expect, and now it is a dispute instead of a payment. The layer prepares the plain-language explanation before the invoice lands.

Boundaries

RevvOS is not a payment processor.

GlassBiller already handles the money movement and the books. RevvOS does not process payments, advance funds, or replace the platform's accounting. It reads the state of the job and the balance, prepares the follow-up that keeps money moving, and writes the result back. The customer's dollar still travels the same rails it always did.

The exact connection depends on GlassBiller's current interfaces and your configuration, which we confirm against your setup.

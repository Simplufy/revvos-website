Integration

QuickBooks is right about the past. The bay is where the future is decided.

QuickBooks is excellent accounting software, and shops use it well: it knows what was invoiced, paid, and deposited. But it only knows what has been posted to it. Between the repair order and the journal entry, work happens that never makes it into the books until someone remembers to key it in, and money that should be chased sits exactly in that space.

Four Questions Owners Actually Ask

Each question has a place it lives, and a gap.

None of these is exotic. They are the questions that wake an owner up. The trouble is that the accurate answer lives in one system and half the story lives in another.

"Did we actually invoice everything we finished this month?"

In QuickBooks: every invoice that was posted. Accurate, complete, and only as good as the posting discipline.
The gap: a finished repair order that never got invoiced sits in the shop system, invisible to the books. The layer flags finished-but-unbilled work.

"What are we owed, and who do we chase?"

In QuickBooks: the open receivable and its age. The number is there.
The gap: the number does not knock on doors. The layer turns an aged invoice into a prepared, polite, correctly-toned collection message for a person to approve. Collections ->

"Which jobs quietly lost money?"

In QuickBooks: totals by month or account, once everything is classified.
The gap: the books see dollars, not jobs. The layer reads the RO-level view: which job type, which advisor, which declined line turned into a callback that never happened. Profitability ->

"Why does month-end take so long?"

In QuickBooks: the reconciliation itself, waiting on clean, complete entries.
The gap: the delay is upstream. Unposted work and unclassified line items. The layer surfaces the exceptions before close, not during it. Accounting automation ->

Boundaries

RevvOS does not replace your accountant or your ledger.

QuickBooks is the book of record. RevvOS does not post journal entries, file taxes, or make accounting decisions. It reads from the shop system and, where your setup supports it, from QuickBooks, prepares the operational next step that keeps money moving, and writes the result back to the source of truth. Your books stay your books.

The exact connection depends on which QuickBooks edition you run, your existing sync, and your configuration, which we confirm against your setup. Shops that already sync their shop system to QuickBooks keep that sync; the layer reads from it rather than competing with it.

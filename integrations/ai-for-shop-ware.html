Integration

One job, start to finish, across Shop-Ware and the layer above it.

Shop-Ware handles the estimate, the workflow board, the parts, and the customer messages. It even ships its own AI-branded tools. So the honest question is not whether Shop-Ware has automation. It is what happens when a job leaves the shop's own interface and the next step depends on the phone, the calendar, and the customer's history all at once.

Name check

Shop-Ware (hyphen) is shop management. Shopware (no hyphen) is ecommerce.

If you searched "AI for Shop-Ware" and landed on pages about online stores, product pages, and checkout, that is a different product in a different industry. This page is about shop-ware.com, the auto repair shop management platform used by repair shops, not the German ecommerce system. Everything below assumes you run bays, not a webshop.

The Life Of One Job

Five states. Three of them are where shops leak work.

A job does not sit still in a shop management system. It moves through states. Most revenue loss lives in the transitions, not in the states themselves.

STATE 01 - INBOUND

The call or the booking lands.

Shop-Ware's AI Receptionist and online scheduling take the booking. The record is created in the platform.

What the layer adds: the same caller checked against their open work and their last declined line, so the booking is shaped by history the platform holds but does not surface at the moment of answer. Missed call recovery ->

STATE 02 - ESTIMATE SENT

The estimate goes out and goes quiet.

Shop-Ware sends the estimate and records its state. This is the transition where the most work evaporates, because "sent" and "decided" are not the same, and nothing in a record system tells you which silent estimates are alive.

What the layer adds: the estimate tracked by state (sent, seen, approved but not scheduled, partially approved) with a different prepared next step for each state, held for advisor approval. Estimate follow-up ->

STATE 03 - IN THE BAY

The vehicle is in the shop and the DVI finds more.

Shop-Ware runs the digital workflow, the DVI, and the parts. The additional findings become new lines on the estimate, some of which the customer declines.

What the layer adds: a declined line is not a closed line. It becomes a state the layer keeps open and re-surfaces on a real reason, not on a marketing cadence. Declined work follow-up ->

STATE 04 - DELIVERED

The car leaves. The relationship should not.

Shop-Ware's CRM handles thank-you messages, review requests, promotions, and win-back campaigns on its own schedule.

What the layer adds: delivery and reputation signals read together, so a review or a review problem is connected to the specific visit and advisor, not just counted. Reputation ->

STATE 05 - DEMAND

Tomorrow's bays depend on today's demand.

Shop-Ware's Capacity Management shows what the schedule will hold. What it cannot see is the demand side that fills those bays: local search, paid ads, and how many of those calls were never answered.

What the layer adds: spend and search read next to the booked work they produced, so capacity planning and marketing stop living in separate rooms. Marketing and demand ->

The Seam

Automation inside a system reasons about that system.

Shop-Ware's automation is strong, and it is native: reminders on its data, AI pricing on its parts catalog, a receptionist on its number. That is the right design for a system of record. It is also why the seams are seams. The phone event, the estimate state, the review, and the ad spend live in different places, and no single module is positioned to reason across all of them.

RevvOS is that cross-system layer. It reads from Shop-Ware and writes back to it. The estimate stays the estimate. The advisor keeps one interface. Nothing is double-entered, and every prepared action waits for a person to approve it.

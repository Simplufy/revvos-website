Collision Operations

Auto body shop automation software is not your estimating system.

One system keeps the record: the estimate, the repair order, the parts, the money, the insurer paperwork. The other moves work: the tow that lands at 6:40 in the morning, the customer calling for the third time, the calibration sublet nobody has confirmed. A body shop buys both, and they are not the same purchase.

See it on your intake -> Collision overview

Two Ledgers

Every body shop runs two ledgers. Only one of them is software you buy from an estimating vendor.

The record ledger is the industry's home turf. CCC ONE, Mitchell, Audatex and the shop management platforms underneath them hold the estimate version history, the repair order, the parts invoices, the supplement trail and the money. That is a mature category, and it should stay where it is.

Ledger A / The record

What the shop stores

  • The estimate and its revision history
  • The supplement requested and the supplement approved
  • The repair order, the parts lines, the sublet lines
  • Photos, blueprint sheets, OEM procedures on file
  • Insurer, claim number, deductible, betterment
  • Labor, paint materials, and the final bill

This is a system of record. You do not want an AI tool owning any part of it.

Ledger B / The motion

What the shop chases

  • A tow that has been dispatched but not logged
  • An insurer update that is two days stale
  • A part ordered Tuesday with no ETA on Thursday
  • A glass or calibration sublet nobody has booked
  • A supplement that has not been raised yet
  • A car sitting in the wrong stage of production

Nobody sells this as a category, because it is not a database. It is motion, and in most shops it is motion carried in one person's head.

Where RevvOS sits: in ledger B. It reads from your shop management system and your estimating platform, watches the motion, prepares the next step, and writes back. The record stays the record.

The Work That Moves

Six jobs a body shop actually automates.

Each one below is the same shape today: a person is the trigger, the memory, and the follow-up. Automation does not replace the judgment at the end of the job, it removes the person from having to remember the job exists.

01

Tow and intake capture

TRIGGER: TOW DISPATCHED
Today

The tow company calls the shop line. Whoever answers writes the VIN on paper, or forgets. The car appears in the lot and the writer finds out when they walk past.

With a workflow

The tow notice, the insurer referral or the missed call becomes an intake record with VIN, location, drivability and the referring party already attached, waiting for a writer to accept it.

Human owns: accepting the job, the first walk-around, the initial estimate.

02

The customer and insurer update cadence

TRIGGER: DAY SINCE LAST CONTACT
Today

The customer in a rental calls for status and the writer leaves a bay to go look. The insurer learns about a delay when the rental bill shows up.

With a workflow

Every car has a contact clock. A draft update is prepared on the day the shop set, naming the current stage honestly and the next milestone, for a writer to approve and send. The status call is answered before it is made.

Human owns: the message. Nothing goes out promising a completion date the shop has not committed to.

03

Parts ordering and tracking

TRIGGER: PART ON THE ESTIMATE
Today

Someone orders from the estimator screen, then remembers nothing more. The part becomes a problem on the day the car is disassembled and it is not on the shelf.

With a workflow

Every ordered part gets a clock measured against the car's production date. No confirmation, no ETA, wrong part, backorder: each becomes an exception with an owner rather than a discovery.

Human owns: the parts choice, the OEM versus alternative call, and what the insurer will actually pay for.

04

Sublet coordination

TRIGGER: DEPENDENCY ON ANOTHER VENDOR
Today

Glass, alignment, and ADAS calibration are booked by whoever remembers, in whatever order, often after the car is painted. The car waits in the lot and the rental clock keeps running.

With a workflow

Sublets appear on the car's plan as dependencies with a booking state and an owner. An unbooked calibration with the car two days from reassembly raises itself loudly.

Human owns: the vendor relationship and the decision about whether the sublet is even needed. See where calibration belongs in an estimate.

05

Supplement request preparation

TRIGGER: TEARDOWN FINDING
Today

The teardown finding goes into a notepad, a text to the estimator, or nothing. The supplement is assembled from memory days later, and the car pays for the delay in rental days.

With a workflow

Damage photos, the OEM procedure reference, the part number and the labor justification are assembled into a packet the moment the finding is logged, so the estimator starts from a file instead of a blank page.

Human owns: the appraiser call, the negotiation, and the signature. Full workflow at collision supplement automation.

06

Production scheduling across stages

TRIGGER: STAGE CHANGE
Today

The board is updated at the end of the day, if at all. Bottlenecks are found at the morning meeting, which is one day after they formed.

With a workflow

Stage transitions stamp themselves as they happen. A car that has sat in one stage past the shop's own threshold surfaces that morning, with who it is waiting on.

Human owns: the sequencing decision and the tech assignment. See cycle time software.

What To Automate First

Start where the shop is already losing money, not where the software is most impressive.

Body shops that automate everything at once usually end up with three half-configured tools and an advisor who trusts none of them. This order lets each step pay for the next one.

1

Intake capture and the status clock

Automate the two things a customer notices and a writer cannot keep up with: nothing arriving unnoticed, and nobody calling to ask where their car is. Both produce visible evidence in the first month.

Skip it and every later step reports on work that was never captured cleanly.

2

Parts and sublet clocks

These are the two dependencies that stop a car dead, and software can see both: an order with no ETA, a calibration with no booking. Neither needs judgment to detect.

Skip it and cycle time gains from step one get eaten by cars waiting on a shelf or a vendor.

3

Supplement preparation and stage tracking

Do these after the first two, because a supplement packet is only as complete as the photos and the part status behind it. Assembling documentation is the highest-value automation in a shop that is not already doing it.

Skip it and the shop keeps leaving labor and materials unclaimed. A supplement request prepared on the day of the teardown is a different conversation with an appraiser than one raised a week into the rental.

4

Nothing that touches the insurer relationship

None of the steps above negotiate anything. If a vendor pitches a workflow that files, signs, or agrees on your behalf, that is outside the category this page describes, and outside what a DRP relationship survives.

The estimate, the supplement, the appraiser call and the total-loss conversation with a customer stay human. Always.

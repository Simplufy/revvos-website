Auto repair estimate follow up software for estimates still open

Auto repair estimate follow up software should find the estimates nobody has answered, draft a specific message for each and leave the decision to send it with your advisor. It should know an estimate's state, set timing by job size, attach inspection photos and stop the moment the customer replies.

Updated October 16, 2026 · 5 min read

Auto repair estimate follow up software should do one thing well: make sure that every estimate that has not been answered gets a person's attention while the customer is still thinking about it. It should not blast texts. It should find the quiet estimates, draft a message that fits each one, and let your advisor decide whether to send it.

This page is about estimates that are still open, from the moment you send the quote until the customer says yes or no. Once the answer is no, you have a different job, covered in declined work follow up software.

Why open estimates go quiet

Picture a Tuesday. The advisor sends a brake and rotor estimate mid-morning and moves on to three walk-ins. By Thursday the customer has not replied. Nobody decided to drop it. It just stopped being the loudest thing on the screen.

Cox Automotive's 2025 Service Industry Study found 45% of owners dissatisfied with dealer service, "primarily due to unexpected costs and poor communication," and 55% saying comparing costs online is very important. An estimate with no follow-up is a customer doing that comparison without you in the conversation. Shops see the hesitation too: in PartsTech's 2026 survey of nearly 700 shops, 50% said customers are delaying or declining work.

What the software has to do

  1. Know the estimate's state. Sent, viewed, approved, partly approved, expired. If it reads from your shop system, it should say when it last synced.
  2. Set a clock per estimate. Not one rule for everything. A $90 service and a $2,400 repair need different timing.
  3. Draft, don't fire. The message should reference the actual items, in plain words, and leave the advisor free to change it or call instead.
  4. Attach evidence. The 2026 J.D. Power study, as reported by CarPro, found 64% of customers want photo or video evidence of needed work. A follow-up that links the inspection photos answers the question the customer is probably asking.
  5. Stop when the customer answers. Obvious, and the most common failure. A reminder that arrives after the customer already approved by phone looks careless.
  6. Record what happened. Sent, edited, ignored, answered, so you can see over a month what works.

A follow-up sequence you can copy

This is a starting point, not a result. Adjust it with your own numbers.

  • Same day: Make sure the estimate itself is clear: the condition to be repaired, the parts and the labor, in writing. State law may add requirements, so check yours.
  • Day 1 or 2: A short check from the advisor: "Any questions on the estimate? Happy to walk through it." Offer a call.
  • Day 4 or 5: Reference the one item that matters most (the safety item first). Send the photo. Do not repeat the whole estimate.
  • Day 7 or later: One last, low-pressure note that the quote will expire and that you can re-price if parts costs change.

After that, stop. A fifth message is not follow-up. It is the thing that makes people leave a one-star review.

Who sends it, and by what channel

Texting is the usual choice, and it comes with rules. Twilio's documentation says anyone sending SMS or MMS from an application over a 10DLC number to the US must register for A2P 10DLC. A tool that texts for you should tell you how that registration is handled. Ask your own lawyer how customer consent applies to your texts.

The sender should also be a person's name. A message from "Dave at the shop" gets answered more often than one from a no-reply number, and the customer can reply to a human.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Automating the price conversation. If a customer pushes back on cost, a person takes over. We would not let software negotiate.
  • Counting follow-ups instead of outcomes. Messages sent is a vanity number. Estimates answered is the one that matters.
  • Following up on work that needs a diagnosis first. If the estimate is a guess, fix the estimate.
  • Ignoring partial approvals. The customer approved two of four items. Software that marks the estimate "approved" will stop following up on the other two.

Questions people ask

How soon should a shop follow up on an estimate?

A short check one or two days after sending is reasonable, a second note around day four or five and a last one by day seven. Treat that as a starting point and adjust to your own response data.

Should follow-up messages be automatic?

Drafted automatically, sent by a person. A reminder that goes out after the customer already approved by phone looks careless, and price pushback needs a human.

Is texting estimate reminders allowed?

Texting from an application over a 10DLC number requires A2P 10DLC registration, per Twilio. Customer consent rules depend on your situation, so ask your lawyer.

What should an estimate follow-up include?

The specific items, why they matter (safety first) and the inspection photos or video. 64% of customers want that evidence, per J.D. Power as reported by CarPro.

How is this different from declined work follow-up?

Estimate follow-up covers quotes still waiting for an answer. Declined work starts after the customer says no or not now, and needs reason codes and slower timing.

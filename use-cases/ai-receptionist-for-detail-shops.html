Detailing

An AI receptionist for a detail shop has to know a detail call when it hears one.

Detail shops inherit their phone scripts from repair shops, and the scripts do not fit. Nobody is calling about a noise or a check engine light. They are calling about a package, a paint condition, a coating package, or a price they already have from someone else.

See it on your calls -> Detailing overview

The Shape Of The Call

Five things that make a detail call a different animal.

A repair call resolves into a line item. A detail call resolves into a package, a vehicle, and a description of a condition. That single difference changes what has to be captured before anyone can quote anything.

Condition is the price

Two identical SUVs can be a four hour job or a two day job. Pet hair, odor, sap, and neglected paint decide it, and none of them travel down a phone line.

The big tickets are consults

A ceramic coating or paint protection film (PPF) buyer asks six questions before asking for a date. That is a consult, not a booking.

Price shopping is default

Detailing is one of the most compared services in the trades. Most callers already have a number from another shop, usually for a package with different scope.

The bay and the weather

A coating has a cure time. A mobile detail has a driveway. Rain, wind, and pollen move the schedule weekly without anyone cancelling anything.

Repeat, not repair

A detail customer returns on a cadence, or joins a membership. The second visit is worth more than the first, which makes the list, not the invoice, the asset.

The Call Ledger

Seven call types, and what each one needs before it can be closed.

Every row below is a call a detail shop actually answers. The left side is what the receptionist must capture no matter what. The right side is the point where a person has to take it.

01 / ONE TIME

A single detail request

"How much for a full detail on a Tahoe?"

Capture: year, make, and model; which package they mean; what condition they describe (pet hair, spills, odor, heavy soil, sap, tar); whether they need the vehicle back by a date; text consent and the best number.

Route: only if the description exceeds the shop's standard package, which is a condition-based quote and belongs to a person.

02 / HIGH TICKET

A ceramic coating or PPF inquiry

"What does your ceramic coating run?"

Capture: the vehicle; what they want it to solve (gloss, protection, resale, hard water spots); whether the paint has ever been corrected; whether any panel has been repainted; how long they plan to keep it; whether they mean an entry coating, a multi-year coating, or film.

Route: always. This is a consult, and the price depends on the paint, which nobody has looked at yet.

03 / EXISTING CUSTOMER

A maintenance or membership question

"What is included, and how often do I need it?"

Capture: whether they are already a member; the vehicle and its last service date; which tier they are asking about; what they believe they already paid for.

Route: if the question becomes a claim about a previous visit, or a request to change membership terms.

04 / COMPARISON

A price shopper

"What is your cheapest full detail?"

Capture: the vehicle; the package; the condition; what the competing number covers, because a wash and wax and a full correction are not the same line; the date they want.

Route: no. This is the call a receptionist handles best, because it captures scope instead of defending a number.

05 / LOGISTICS

A mobile request

"Do you come to me?"

Capture: address and travel distance; whether there is power and water access; where the vehicle sits (garage, driveway, street); the package; the date window; how much space the job needs.

Route: if access is unclear or the job needs the bay. Never let a mobile job be promised before someone confirms the site.

06 / ACCOUNT

A fleet or dealer account

"We have fourteen vans and a rotating lot."

Capture: fleet size and vehicle types; what they need (recurring wash, interiors, coating, delivery prep); the site or sites; who signs the invoice; the cadence they want; how they currently handle it.

Route: always to the owner or manager. An account is negotiated, not booked from a package sheet.

07 / SCHEDULE

A change or weather reschedule

"It is supposed to rain Thursday. Can we move it?"

Capture: the appointment; the reason; the new window they can accept; whether the vehicle is already on site or mobile; how flexible they are across the week.

Route: if the change empties a same-day slot or the vehicle is already in the bay, because someone has to decide how the day reflows.

Escalation Rules

Three calls a detail shop should never let a machine close.

The receptionist is a capture and routing layer. It is not the estimator, and it is not the detailer. The value it adds is that a person picks up a call that already has the vehicle, the condition, the package, and the history attached, instead of starting from "so, tell me about the car."

Three conversations resist automation for structural reasons, not for technical ones.

THE PAINT CORRECTION QUOTE

Correction is priced on the paint, measured under a light, not on what the customer says about it. A description cannot set the price of a multi stage correction.

THE COATING AND FILM SALE

This is an education-led consult that ends in an inspection. It is a conversation about prep, maintenance, and expectations, and it converts on trust rather than on a number.

THE FLEET ACCOUNT

Terms, cadence, and volume pricing belong to an owner. A receptionist captures the shape of the account and hands it over.

Hard Limits

What it must never do.

These are the failure modes that turn a helpful answering layer into a liability. Every one of them is a promise the shop then has to keep or walk back.

Quote a correction job from a description.

The number would be a guess, and the customer would hold it.

Promise that a swirl, scratch, or stain will come out.

Some will not, and the shop cannot un-say it after the vehicle is in the bay.

Promise a delivery time that ignores a cure time.

A coated vehicle cannot leave on a wash cadence, and a cure time is not negotiable because someone has a flight.

Book a mobile detail without confirming the site.

No power, no water, or no space turns a booked job into a wasted drive.

Speak to a claim about prior work or existing damage.

That conversation goes to a person, with the photos and the RO in front of them.

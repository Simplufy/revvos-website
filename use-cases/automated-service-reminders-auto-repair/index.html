Automated service reminders auto repair shops can set up this week

Automated service reminders auto repair shops send should each have a reason (an appointment, a service interval or a declined recommendation), a channel and a named person who owns the reply. Confirmations and pre-written interval reminders can go out on their own; prices, safety items and upset customers stay with an advisor.

Updated October 21, 2026 · 5 min read

A customer's oil change was due three weeks ago. Nobody at the shop knows, and the customer has forgotten. That is the whole problem automated service reminders are meant to solve, and it is smaller and more fixable than most shops treat it.

This page covers what to remind people about, when, over which channel, and which parts are safe to automate. It is for shop owners and service managers who already have a shop management system.

What a reminder is actually for

A reminder is a prompt tied to a reason. The reason is either a date (the appointment is tomorrow), a service interval (the last oil change was six months ago) or an open recommendation (the rear brakes were declined in March). If you cannot name the reason, it is a newsletter, not a reminder.

Drivers do put off maintenance. The Auto Care Association reported, via a survey of more than 100,000 households, that Americans had $24.9 billion in delayed auto maintenance (reported in 2019, so treat the dollar figure as a signal, not a current number). Some of that is money. Some of it is simply nobody asking.

The four reminder types worth setting up

Reminder Trigger Who owns the reply
Appointment confirmation and pre-visit Booking created, then a set time before Front desk
Service interval Date or mileage since last visit Service advisor
Declined or deferred work Recommendation not approved Service advisor
Post-visit check-in A few days after pickup Service manager

Shop systems already do much of this. Tekmetric's own page lists appointment confirmations, pre-visit reminders, post-visit follow-ups, and service reminders for declined jobs and upcoming maintenance, by text, email or both. Check your system's feature list before you buy anything new. The gap is usually not sending. It is deciding who handles the answer.

Pick the reason before the channel

Text is the channel customers tend to like. A TEXT2DRIVE survey reported by Auto Remarketing found 57% of dealership service customers preferred text for appointment reminders. It came from a vendor, covered dealership customers and dates to 2022, so ask your own customers before you rebuild a process around it.

A simple default: text for same-week appointments, email for interval reminders that carry detail, a phone call from a person for anything with a safety item attached.

Set the timing

Start conservative and adjust from your own results.

  1. Appointment reminder: one the day before, one the morning of if the shop runs tight.
  2. Service interval: one when the interval comes due, one four weeks later. Stop there.
  3. Declined work: one soon after the decline for safety items, a second at the next natural point (a season change, the registration month).
  4. Post-visit: one, three to five days after pickup, asking whether the car is running right.

These are starting points, not benchmarks. No number here comes from a study, so test them against your own repair orders.

What to automate and what a person keeps

Safe to send on its own: appointment confirmations, pre-visit reminders, "your vehicle is ready," and interval reminders whose wording you approved ahead of time.

Keep with a person: anything with a price, a safety recommendation, or a reply from a customer who is annoyed or confused. A reminder that says "your brakes are overdue" lands very differently depending on who the customer is and what the last visit felt like. The advisor who knows should read it first.

Consent and registration

Marketing-type messages need the right consent, and texting from software over a standard number needs registration. Twilio's documentation says SMS sent from an application over a 10-digit number must be registered for A2P 10DLC. The FCC's rule on one-to-one consent was vacated in 2025 and the earlier rules reinstated. Where the line falls between a service message and marketing depends on the facts and your state. This page is not legal advice; have a lawyer confirm your wording. Our guide to AI text messaging for auto repair covers the inbox side.

Also give people a way out. STOP should work on every automated channel, and the shop should honor it the same day.

The mistakes that make reminders worse

  • Sending to everyone. A customer who sold the car last year should not get an oil-change reminder. Clean the list first.
  • No owner for replies. The customer texts "Can I come Thursday?" and the message sits in a system nobody watches.
  • Reminder fatigue. More than two or three a year per customer about non-urgent items and people start ignoring all of them.
  • Generic wording. "Your vehicle is due for service" says nothing. Name the car and the item.

Questions people ask

What are automated service reminders for auto repair?

Messages sent by your shop system or another tool when a trigger fires: a booked appointment, a service interval coming due, or a recommendation the customer declined. They go by text, email or both.

How often should an auto shop send service reminders?

Start with one at the interval due date and one about four weeks later, then stop. Appointment reminders are separate. These are starting points to test, not benchmarks.

Do service reminder texts need customer consent?

Marketing-style texts generally need prior express written consent under the TCPA, and service messages are treated differently. The line depends on facts and your state, so have a lawyer confirm your wording.

Does my shop management system already send reminders?

Often yes. Tekmetric lists appointment confirmations, pre-visit reminders, post-visit follow-ups and service reminders for declined jobs and maintenance. Check your system's feature list first.

The Same Hour Follow-Up: windshield quote follow-up templates for glass shops

The Same Hour Follow-Up is a windshield quote follow-up method for glass shops: send the message within the hour of the call, carry the exact glass for the caller's VIN and the same total you quoted, and offer a time you can really hold, without ever promising an insurance outcome. Copy the three message templates, the rule for each case and the weekly check below.

Updated October 21, 2026 · 6 min read

The Same Hour Follow-Up is a way to win back windshield leads that didn't book on the call. It is the same three moves as the video, in the same order:

  1. Send it within the hour, not the next morning, because the caller is comparing shops on the same part number right now.
  2. Carry the exact glass for their VIN and the same total you quoted, so the follow-up continues the sale instead of restarting it.
  3. Offer a time you can really hold, like the van coming to them this afternoon.

And one rule runs through all three: the follow-up never promises an insurance outcome. Below are the three message templates, when to use each, what has to be true before you send, and how to tell it's working.

Why the hour after the call matters

A windshield lead isn't like a mechanical repair lead. It's urgent (a crack is an unsafe commute, a failed inspection or a lease turn-in on Friday), it's price-comparable (two shops quoting the same part for the same vehicle are quoting nearly the same job), and it's usually decided on the first call. So the follow-up is a recovery tool, not a nurture sequence.

Speed of response is a known weak spot well beyond glass. Researchers writing in Harvard Business Review about online sales leads found that most companies are not responding nearly fast enough. For a windshield, "fast enough" means before the caller books with the next shop on their list. Our longer page on auto glass lead follow-up software covers the background.

Who it is for

  • Independent glass shops whose front desk quotes on the phone and loses some callers who were "just checking prices".
  • Mobile shops where the real objection is often logistics, not money.
  • Owners who want follow-up automated but don't want a robot promising things about insurance.

What you need first

  • A quote record for every call: the caller, the vehicle, the VIN, the part number and the total you gave.
  • Tomorrow's and today's van schedule, so you can see real open slots.
  • A short list of the three reasons quotes stall at your shop (see below).
  • A person who approves each follow-up before it goes out, at least for the first month.

Step 1. Send it within the hour

Set a rule: any quote that ended without a booking gets a follow-up drafted within the hour of the call, and someone approves it the same hour. If the call came in late in the afternoon, it goes out that evening, not the next morning.

Three cases are worth chasing, and each stalls for a different reason:

  • The quote that wasn't accepted. They heard the price and said they'd think about it.
  • The caller who was plainly shopping. They asked for a price and hung up.
  • The open insurance question. They didn't know how their claim works and left without an answer.

Step 2. Carry the exact glass and the same total

A "just checking in" message does nothing for a windshield. The follow-up has to repeat the specifics the customer is comparing: the part that fits their VIN, whether it's OEM or aftermarket, and the same total you quoted. A follow-up that reopens the price restarts the sale.

Template A: the quote that wasn't accepted

Hi [first name], it's [shop] following up on your [year model] windshield. The glass that fits your VIN ending [last 4] is [part number] ([OEM or aftermarket]), and your total is still [quoted total], the same as on the phone. We can come to you [day] at [time]. Reply YES and we'll hold it.

Template B: the caller who was shopping

Hi [first name], thanks for calling [shop] about your [year model]. For your VIN the correct windshield is [part number], and our total is [quoted total] including [what's included]. If it helps, our van can do it at your home or work [day] at [time].

Template C: the open insurance question

Hi [first name], it's [shop]. You asked how a glass claim works. Here's what to have ready: your policy number, the date the damage happened and a photo of the crack. We can help you start the claim, and your insurer decides what's covered. We have [day] at [time] open if you'd like to book.

Step 3. Offer a time you can really hold

The reason a glass lead stalls is often logistics: the customer can't get the car to you or can't take a morning off. Offering the van is the answer to the objection they didn't say out loud. But only offer a slot someone has checked. A follow-up that names a time nobody can keep costs you the customer twice.

When you check the slot, remember the stop isn't over when the glass is set. I-CAR says minimum drive-away time may be as little as 30-60 minutes for fast-cure products and 2-8 hours or longer for moisture-cured ones, and AGRR reported that most major adhesive makers now offer a 30-minute product. Tell the customer how long before they can drive, using the product on your van.

The rule: never promise an insurance outcome

A follow-up can remind, restate a price, offer a time and hand a claim question to a person. It can't promise that a claim will be approved, that a deductible will be waived or that the carrier will pay a figure, because your shop doesn't control any of that. Coverage really does vary: the Insurance Information Institute notes that some policies include no-deductible glass coverage and others need it added separately.

Follow-up is where shops are most tempted to say something generous to close a job, and an automated message that oversells coverage becomes a promise someone has to keep at the counter. Block phrases like "your insurance will cover this", "no cost to you" and "we'll waive your deductible" in any tool that writes to customers.

What to watch out for

  • Don't send the same message to all three cases. Each stalled for a different reason.
  • Don't change the total in the follow-up. If the price really changed, a person calls.
  • Don't automate the approval at first. Read every draft for a month, then decide.

How to tell it worked

Each week, pull every quote that didn't book on the call and answer three questions: how many got a follow-up within the hour, how many of those booked, and whether any message mentioned an insurance outcome. The third answer should always be zero. If you'd like those unbooked quotes and their follow-ups in one view, start with the Multi-Shop Operating Gap Assessment. Questions people ask

How fast should a glass shop follow up on a windshield quote?

Within the hour of the call. Windshield leads are urgent and price-comparable, so the caller is usually deciding the same day.

What should a windshield follow-up message include?

The part that fits the caller's VIN, whether it's OEM or aftermarket, the same total you quoted on the phone, and a time slot someone has actually checked.

Can a follow-up say insurance will cover the windshield?

No. The shop doesn't control the claim and coverage varies by policy, so a follow-up can explain the claim steps but never promise approval, a waived deductible or a payment amount.

Should I send the same follow-up to every lost quote?

No. A quote that wasn't accepted, a caller who was shopping and an open insurance question stalled for different reasons, so each gets its own message.

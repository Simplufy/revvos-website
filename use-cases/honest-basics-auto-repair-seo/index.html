The Honest Basics: a monthly auto repair SEO checklist for AI search

The Honest Basics is an auto repair SEO checklist that gets a shop ready for Google's AI answers without any tricks, because Google says no special optimization is needed to appear in them. Each month, make your listing match reality, keep one real page for every service you perform, and let AI audit the gaps while a person at the shop fixes them. The checks, a service page outline and what to skip are below.

Updated October 21, 2026 · 6 min read

The Honest Basics is a monthly checklist that gets a repair shop ready for Google's AI answers without buying any tricks. It has the same three checks as the video, in the same order:

  1. Your listing matches reality: the hours, phone number and categories your shop really runs.
  2. One real page for every service you perform, written with your own turnaround times and the brands you work on.
  3. An AI audit that a person acts on: AI flags wrong hours and missing pages, and someone at the shop fixes them.

Below is why these three are enough, what you need before you start, the exact checks to run each month, and what to skip.

Why the basics are the whole job

Google's guidance on its AI features is plain: there are no additional requirements to appear in them, nor other special optimizations necessary. A page has to be indexed and eligible to show in Search with a snippet to be used as a supporting link, and that's it. There is no special AI markup to add.

So when someone offers a secret "AI search package", you're being sold something Google says you don't need. The work that does help is the same work that always helped local shops, and AI mostly makes it faster. Our longer page on AI SEO for auto repair shops explains the background.

Who it is for

  • Owners of independent shops who have a few hours a month for their online presence.
  • Multi-shop groups that want the same check run at every location.
  • Anyone who has been pitched "AI search optimization" and wants to know what to do instead.

What you need first

  • Owner or manager access to each location's Google Business Profile.
  • A list of every service you actually perform at each location, and the ones you don't.
  • Your real hours, including Saturday and holiday hours, written down by someone who works the counter.
  • Access to Google Search Console for your website, if you have it set up.

Check 1. Make the listing match reality

Google says complete and accurate information is more likely to show in local results, and that local results rest on relevance, distance and prominence. Wrong hours are the most common quiet mistake, because nobody looks at the listing once it's set up.

Each month, at each location:

  1. Compare the listed hours with the hours on the door, day by day. Saturday and holidays are where they drift.
  2. Call the listed phone number from a cell phone. Make sure it rings at that location.
  3. Read the categories. The main one should describe what you mostly do (for example, auto repair shop), with extras only for work you really offer.
  4. Check the address pin on the map and the website link.
  5. Fix anything wrong the same day, and write down what changed.

Check 2. One real page per service

List every service you perform and check whether each one has a page on your site that answers what a customer asks before booking: what the job involves, how long it usually takes at your shop, what the estimate covers, which makes you work on and how to book.

Google's SEO Starter Guide asks for helpful, reliable, people-first content and page titles that are unique and describe the page. A service page that says "We do brakes" in one line doesn't meet that. A page that says your usual brake turnaround is same day or next day, lists the makes you see most, and shows a real photo of your bay does.

AI can help here. Google says generative AI can be particularly useful when researching a topic and to add structure to original content. Let it draft an outline from your notes, then fill in the facts only your shop knows. The turnaround, the brands and the photos have to come from you.

A service page outline you can copy:

  • What the service is, in one plain sentence.
  • When a driver usually needs it (the noise, the light, the mileage).
  • What the inspection and estimate include at your shop.
  • Your usual turnaround range, in your own words.
  • The makes you work on most.
  • How to book, with the phone number for that location.
  • Three questions customers ask at the counter, with your answers.

Check 3. Let AI audit, and let a person fix

This is where AI saves the most time. Once a month, have it compare three things side by side: your Business Profile, your website and your real schedule. Ask it to list mismatched hours, services with no page, pages for services you've stopped doing and missing phone numbers.

It can also read your reviews. In BrightLocal's 2026 survey, 97% of consumers read reviews for local businesses. An AI summary that groups last month's reviews by theme (wait time, communication, price) shows you which page needs a better answer and which problem belongs in the shop.

The rule: the AI flags, a person decides. Nobody should let a tool change hours, categories or pages on its own.

What to skip

  • A page for every suburb. Google's spam policies treat city pages that funnel people to one place as doorway abuse, and many pages made without adding value as scaled content abuse. Build a location page only where you have a shop.
  • Fake reviews and bought links. They put the whole listing at risk.
  • Anyone who promises a ranking. Google says a change can take anywhere from a few hours to several months to show, and nobody controls the result.
  • Special AI markup. There isn't any to add. If you use structured data, it should match what's visible on the page.

How to tell it worked

Run the three checks for three months and track your own numbers, not a ranking promise:

  • The number of wrong items each audit finds should fall month by month.
  • Every service you perform should have a real page by the end of the second month.
  • In Search Console, appearances in AI features are counted in the normal Web search type, so watch the queries that bring impressions and clicks to your service pages.
  • At the counter, ask new customers how they found you and keep a simple tally.

Judge the result by calls, bookings and reviews. If you'd like the listing, page and review checks run for every shop in one view, the Multi-Shop Operating Gap Assessment is where to start.

Questions people ask

Do I need special SEO to show up in Google's AI Overviews?

No. Google says there are no additional requirements or special optimizations to appear in its AI features. A page needs to be indexed and eligible to show with a snippet, so the ordinary basics are the job.

Should I make a page for every town near my shop?

No. Google's spam policies treat city pages that funnel visitors to one place as doorway abuse. Make a location page only where you actually have a shop.

Can AI write my service pages?

It can draft an outline and structure from your notes. The facts, like your turnaround times, the makes you work on and your photos, have to come from your shop.

How often should a repair shop check its Google listing?

Once a month at each location, and any time your hours change. Saturday and holiday hours are where listings drift most often.

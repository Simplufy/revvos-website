The Glass Work Order Map: an auto glass work order checklist with AI in the right places

The Glass Work Order Map is an auto glass work order checklist with five lines every windshield passes before the car leaves: the VIN and the part, the quote, the schedule, the calibration and the paperwork. At each line it shows what a system can prepare and what a person owns, with the fields, quote line, sign-off record and claim checklist to copy.

Updated October 21, 2026 · 9 min read

The Glass Work Order Map is one auto glass work order checklist with five lines, and every windshield passes all five before the car leaves:

  1. The VIN and the part. Decode the VIN, narrow the glass to the right part, and flag any camera or sensor behind it.
  2. The quote. Put the calibration on its own line, on the insurance path and the cash path alike.
  3. The schedule. Book the van or the bay with the calibration time already blocked, static or dynamic.
  4. The calibration. A qualified tech follows the OEM procedure and signs off. Software never does this step.
  5. The paperwork. The carrier's document checklist and the calibration record travel together, and the customer gets an update.

At each line there's a part a system can prepare and a part a person owns. The rest of this sheet goes deeper on each one, with the fields, the quote line, the scheduling rule, the sign-off record and the paperwork list you can copy into whatever you use today.

Who this sheet is for

It's written for owners and office leads at auto glass shops, windshield replacement companies and mobile glass operations, from one van to a few locations. It also works for a general repair or collision shop that does glass in-house. If your team already writes good glass quotes, the map still helps, because the steps that slip are usually the ones between people: the calibration the quote missed, the van booked without calibration time, or the claim file waiting on a document nobody asked for.

You don't need new software to start. A printed copy of the five lines, clipped to each work order for two weeks, will show you where your jobs leak.

What you need before you start

  • Your record system's work order screen. Whatever you write glass jobs in, know where the VIN, the part, the quote lines and the notes live.
  • Access to calibration requirements. I-CAR's OEM Calibration Requirements Search covers 2016 to 2024 models and shows which events require calibration, where the sensors sit and whether special tools are needed. Your OEM service information is the final word.
  • Your own rules in writing. Your drive-away time for each adhesive and temperature, who on your team is qualified to calibrate, and which carriers or administrators you work with.
  • One owner. Pick one person who reviews the five lines on every closed job for two weeks.

Line 1. The VIN and the part

The VIN feels like it should settle the glass. It doesn't. An AGRR magazine article on glass lookups points out that the VIN does not include the glass part number at all, and that one model can carry up to 16 different windshields with 16 different NAGS numbers. Trim, rain sensor, heads-up display, acoustic laminate and the camera package all change the part.

What a system can prepare: the VIN decode, a short candidate list of parts, and a flag on any part that sits in front of a camera or sensor.

What a person owns: which part is quoted, which part is installed, and the OEM versus aftermarket conversation with the customer.

Intake fields to copy onto every glass work order:

Field Example (Redline Auto Glass, demo)
VIN captured from the customer or a photo of the plate on the dash
Year, make, model, trim a recent SUV, mid trim
Glass features rain sensor, acoustic, forward camera
Candidate parts two part numbers, with the notes on each
Calibration flag yes, forward camera
Chosen part and who chose it filled in by the estimator

Line 2. The quote

This is where the money leaks. In AAA's ADAS repair cost study of three 2023 models, the windshield scenario added $360 to transfer and calibrate the windshield-mounted camera to new glass, and that figure covers the ADAS work alone. A quote that leaves calibration out is either a cost the shop absorbs or a surprise the customer hears at pickup.

What a system can prepare: the quote assembled from the VIN and part data, the calibration line added whenever Line 1 raised the flag, and both payment paths shown side by side.

What a person owns: the price, the part sourcing behind it, and any terms the shop has agreed with a carrier.

A quote line you can copy:

Camera calibration after windshield replacement, per the vehicle maker's procedure. Method: static / dynamic / both. Included in the insurance estimate: yes / no. Included in the cash price: yes / no.

If either "Included" answer is blank, the quote isn't ready to send.

Line 3. The schedule

Calibration decides the shape of the appointment. I-CAR describes two kinds of aiming for forward cameras: static, done in the shop with targets in front of the vehicle and a scan tool, and dynamic, where the vehicle is driven down the road at a set speed for a set time or distance. Some cameras need both, and removing the windshield is one of the events that can trigger it.

That changes how you book a mobile job. A driveway might be fine for the glass and wrong for a static calibration, so the work order needs a plan for where and when the calibration happens before the van leaves.

What a system can prepare: the route, your drive-away time for that adhesive and temperature, a calibration slot in the bay or a road window, and access notes for the site.

What a person owns: whether a site is suitable to work in, and every promise to the customer about when the car is ready.

Scheduling rules to write down once: - A calibration flag on Line 1 means no job is booked without a calibration slot. - Static calibration: book the bay and the target setup time with the install, or the trip back to the shop. - Dynamic calibration: book road time after the drive-away time has passed. - The customer hears one ready time, and it includes the calibration.

Line 4. The calibration

This is the line that decides whether the car is safe to drive, and it belongs to a qualified person every time. I-CAR's guidance is plain: always refer to OEM procedures for how to calibrate the system. The industry's replacement standard agrees. The 2022 update to the AGRSS standard added sections on static and dynamic calibration and on calibration equipment requirements, including compatibility with each vehicle maker.

What a system can prepare: the calibration requirement attached to that VIN and part, the time already blocked, and a reminder on the work order so nobody finds out at pickup.

What a person owns: the calibration, the choice of method, and the sign-off that the vehicle can go back on the road.

A sign-off record to copy:

Field Filled in by
Work order number system
Requirement source (OEM procedure or lookup) system, checked by the tech
Method performed (static, dynamic, both) tech
Equipment used tech
Result and any warning lights after tech
Tech name, date and time tech
Printout or scan report attached tech

Line 5. The paperwork

On an insurance job the shop is often waiting on someone else. Many carriers send glass claims through a third-party administrator, and Repairer Driven News reported in 2025 that State Farm moved its glass claims administration to Safelite Solutions, which a trade group describes as the administrator for over 200 insurance and fleet companies. Each one has its own portal and document rules.

The calibration record belongs in that file too. AAA's advice to drivers is to request proof that safety systems were properly repaired and calibrated, so a shop that hands it over without being asked looks like the shop that did the job right.

What a system can prepare: the document checklist for that carrier, the age of every open file, a draft customer update, and the invoice with the calibration record attached.

What a person owns: every submission, every conversation with an adjuster or an administrator, and any decision about money owed. Software never says what insurance will pay.

Paperwork checklist per job: - Claim or reference number, policyholder name and the deductible the customer confirmed. - Photos of the damage before work. - Part number installed, and the invoice. - The signed calibration record from Line 4. - The date the file was submitted, and the date of the last answer.

A customer update you can copy and edit:

Hi, it's Redline Auto Glass. Your new windshield is in, and the camera behind it has been calibrated and checked. Your paperwork, including the calibration report, is ready, and you can pick the car up this afternoon.

What to watch out for

  • Software that sounds sure about coverage. No draft should say what a carrier will pay or whether a chip is repairable. Those answers belong to the carrier and your technician.
  • A drive-away time made up on the spot. The system repeats your written rule for that adhesive and temperature. It never invents one.
  • Calibration found at pickup. If a car reaches pickup with the calibration flag still open, the gap is in the quote or the schedule, not in the bay.
  • A mobile booking with no calibration plan. Check every van job for its calibration slot before it leaves the yard.

How to tell it worked

After two weeks, pull ten closed glass jobs and score each line yes or no: part matched, calibration on the quote, calibration time booked, signed record attached, paperwork complete. A job with five yes answers left the shop finished, not just installed. Count the no answers per line, and fix the line with the most first.

Questions people ask

Does every windshield replacement need a camera calibration?

Not every one, but removing the windshield is one of the events that can require aiming a forward camera, and the vehicle maker's procedure decides. Check the OEM service information or I-CAR's OEM Calibration Requirements Search for that year, make and model, and record the answer on the work order.

Can AI calibrate the camera or decide the method?

No. Software can attach the requirement to the work order, add the line to the quote and block the time. A qualified technician performs the calibration with the right equipment, chooses static, dynamic or both per the OEM procedure, and signs off.

Why can't I pick the windshield from the VIN alone?

The VIN does not include the glass part number, and one model can have many windshield options with different features. Decode the VIN, then confirm features like the rain sensor, heads-up display and camera before you order.

Where should the calibration record go?

In the work order and in the claim file, and a copy to the customer. AAA tells drivers to ask for proof their safety systems were properly repaired and calibrated, so handing it over unasked is good service.

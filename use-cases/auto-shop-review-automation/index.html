Auto shop review automation: ask every customer, break no rules

Auto shop review automation works when it asks every customer the same way, after the car has been picked up, with no incentive and no sorting by expected rating. Google bans gating and incentives, the FTC's rule covers bought and fake reviews, and a person should still write replies to complaints.

Updated October 21, 2026 · 5 min read

A customer picks up a car, says thanks, and walks out happy. Ninety seconds later the shop has forgotten to ask for a review, and the customer will never think of it again. Auto shop review automation exists to close that gap. It also has a few ways to go wrong that can get your reviews removed or your shop in front of the FTC.

This page covers how to set up a review request flow that is allowed, what to automate, and what a person should still write. It is for shop owners and service managers.

Why reviews are worth the effort

Shoppers read them. BrightLocal's 2026 survey of 1,002 US adults found that 97% of consumers read reviews for local businesses, and 74% look for reviews written in the last three months. That second number is the real argument for automation: a pile of old reviews does not help if nothing new has come in since spring.

Google also says that more reviews and positive ratings can help your business's local ranking, alongside relevance and distance.

What you may ask, and what you may not

Google's policy is plain. Shops may solicit or encourage reviews that represent a genuine experience. They may not:

  • offer incentives such as payment, discounts or free goods or services for a review
  • discourage negative reviews, or selectively ask only happy customers
  • require or pressure people to write one on the premises
  • set quotas for staff or ask for specific content

The second one catches many review tools. A flow that texts everyone, then routes five-star ratings to Google and low ratings to a private form, is what Google calls selective solicitation. Send the same Google link to every customer you ask. A private feedback option can sit beside it, but it cannot replace it.

The FTC's Consumer Reviews and Testimonials rule, announced in August 2024 and effective October 21, 2024, goes further on fake and bought reviews. Per Morgan Lewis's summary, it bars writing or buying reviews that misrepresent who the reviewer is or whether they used the product, and bars offering compensation for a review expressing a particular sentiment, expressly or by implication. So no "leave us five stars for a free oil change." The FTC rule alone would not make every discount illegal, but Google's policy above does bar it.

The flow to automate

  1. Trigger: vehicle picked up and invoice closed, not "work completed." The customer has to have actually left with the car.
  2. Delay: a few hours to a day. Your shop system decides what it allows; test what your customers respond to.
  3. Channel: text or email with one direct link to your Google review page. One ask, no incentive, no star-count wording.
  4. One reminder at most, and not to customers who already left a review.
  5. Flag, don't filter. If the repair order has an unresolved complaint, a manager calls the customer first to fix the problem. The request still goes out on the usual schedule. Withholding it from unhappy customers is selective solicitation, so no one is dropped from the list because of how the visit went.

Shop systems already offer much of this. Tekmetric's page lists post-visit follow-ups that include review requests and private feedback requests. Check what yours does before adding a new tool.

Replies are not the same job

A request brings reviews in. A reply is what the next reader sees. BrightLocal found that 89% of consumers expect business owners to respond to reviews. Automating the nudge is fine. Automating the reply to a complaint is where shops look robotic.

A workable split: positive reviews get a short, specific thank-you that a person can approve in one click. Anything three stars or lower goes to a manager with the repair order attached. Google requires the business profile to be verified to reply, and replies can take time to appear.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Gating by rating. Different links for different moods.
  • Sending too soon. Before the customer has driven the car.
  • Asking everyone twice. One reminder is plenty.
  • No owner. Someone should read new reviews daily.
  • Quotas. "Get ten reviews this week" pushes advisors toward pressure.

Questions people ask

What is auto shop review automation?

Software that sends a review request to customers after a visit, usually by text or email with a direct Google link. It can also route new reviews to the right person, but replies to complaints should come from a human.

Can I offer a discount for a Google review?

No. Google's policy says businesses may not offer incentives such as payment, discounts, free goods or services for reviews. The FTC's 2024 rule also bars compensation for a review that expresses a particular sentiment.

Is sending happy customers to Google and unhappy ones to a private form allowed?

Google's policy bars selectively soliciting positive reviews from customers. Send the same link to everyone you ask. A private feedback option can sit alongside it, not instead of it.

When should a shop send the review request?

After the customer has picked up the car and the invoice is closed, a few hours to a day later. Test the delay against your own results, and have a manager call first if the repair order has an open complaint. The request still goes to every customer.

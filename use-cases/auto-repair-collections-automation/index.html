Auto repair collections automation: invoices, aging and reminders

Auto repair collections automation is software that sends invoices the day a job closes, tracks aging balances and drafts reminders for a person to review, so unpaid fleet and commercial invoices do not drift past 60 days. The phone call, any decision to hold work, and anything legal stay with a person.

Updated October 24, 2026 · 6 min read

Most retail repair work gets paid before the keys go back. Collections is a different problem, and it shows up with fleets, commercial accounts, insurance-adjacent work, and the occasional customer who says the check is in the mail. If your shop carries any of that, auto repair collections automation is about one thing: making sure an unpaid invoice gets a polite, timely nudge without a service manager chasing it by hand.

Why collections slips in a shop

Nobody opens a shop to chase money. The advisor is on the phone, the manager is checking parts ETAs, and the invoice that went out on a net-30 account is easy to forget until it is two months old. ShopView's cash flow guide for heavy-duty shops makes a point that applies to any shop with terms: the payment clock starts when the job is finished, not when the invoice is sent, so it recommends invoicing the same day the job closes.

Terms matter too. The same article notes that net-30 or net-60 invoicing delays cash even on completed work. A trade-press piece on fleet service adds that payments on fleet accounts often run on a 60-day cycle and that one fleet shop owner typically carried about 150,000 dollars in outstanding payments from large accounts. That article is from 2013, so read it as a pattern, not a current benchmark.

What can be automated

Collections has a few steps that are the same every time, which makes them good candidates:

  1. Invoice out the same day. A digital invoice goes to the account contact when the repair order closes, with the terms printed on it. ShopView recommends digital invoices so customers receive bills by email immediately.
  2. An aging report you actually look at. The same guide lists accounts receivable aging, with balances 30, 60 and 90 or more days overdue, as a key metric, and says shop software should track it by customer. It also recommends following up on aging receivables weekly rather than monthly.
  3. Scheduled reminders. A short email before the due date, one on the due date and one after. Each is a draft a person can edit, sent from the shop's real address.
  4. A list for the human. When an invoice passes a threshold you set, it lands on one person's list with the customer, the amount, the age and the last contact. The call is a person's job.

What should not be automated

  • Deciding to hold work or drop a customer. Fullbay's guide lists mechanic's liens and collection agencies as last resorts. Agencies commonly take 20% to 50% of what you charged, and a lien process is long, has many deadlines, varies by state, and the guide recommends consulting a lawyer.
  • Tone with a long-time customer. A person who has sent you ten vehicles a year should hear from someone who knows them.
  • Anything that looks like a third party is collecting. Be careful here. The CFPB's Regulation F says the term debt collector includes any creditor that, in collecting its own debts, uses a name other than its own that would indicate a third person is collecting. Send reminders as your shop, under your name, from your real address. This is general information, not legal advice, and state laws may add rules.

Controls that prevent most collections work

Prevention beats chasing. The sources point to a short list:

  • Credit checks and limits on new accounts, which ShopView says to set and enforce.
  • A cap on total exposure. A consultant quoted by Ratchet+Wrench advises setting a total dollar amount for outstanding payments the business can handle, and no single account above 20 percent of fleet business.
  • Deposits on big jobs. ShopView suggests collecting 50% upfront on jobs over 2,000 to 3,000 dollars and billing progress payments on multi-day jobs.
  • Signed estimates and authorizations, and no personal checks from unknown customers. Fullbay recommends keeping signed estimates and authorizations as documentation.
  • Bounced checks have their own path. The SBA notes that recovery options vary by location and may include small claims court or a collection agency.

A simple weekly rhythm

  1. Monday: open the aging report, oldest first.
  2. Send or approve the reminders that are due.
  3. Call anything past your threshold. Write down what the customer said and when they promised payment.
  4. Put the promised date on the calendar and let the system check it.
  5. Once a month, review credit limits and which accounts paid late.

Questions people ask

What is auto repair collections automation?

Software that sends invoices promptly, tracks aging balances, drafts reminders for review and puts overdue accounts on a person's list. It does not replace the phone call or the decision to hold work.

When should a shop use a collection agency?

Fullbay's guide treats agencies as a last resort and notes fees commonly run 20% to 50% of the original charge, and recovery is not certain. Consider payment plans and direct contact first.

Can I hold a customer's vehicle for unpaid work?

A mechanic's lien can sometimes be used to recover unpaid repair costs, but the process is long, has deadlines and varies by state. Talk to a lawyer before relying on it.

Does the Fair Debt Collection Practices Act apply to my shop?

The CFPB's Regulation F counts a creditor as a debt collector if it uses a name other than its own that suggests a third person is collecting. Send reminders under your own shop name, and check your state's rules. This is not legal advice.

How often should I review receivables?

ShopView recommends following up on aging receivables weekly instead of monthly, and tracking balances that are 30, 60 and 90 or more days overdue.

Does RevvOS collect payments?

No. RevvOS does not process payments or send collection notices by itself. It surfaces items that need a person, with their source and last-updated time.

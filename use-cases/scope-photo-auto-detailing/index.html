The Scope Photo: an auto detailing inspection checklist before you price the job

The Scope Photo is an auto detailing inspection checklist that takes four photos and a note at the car before the price is final: the hood, the seats, the cargo area and the roof. Any gap against the quote gets flagged so you revise before you start, and the person at the car approves the final price. Copy the checklist, the revision message and the setup that runs without being asked below.

Updated October 21, 2026 · 6 min read

The Scope Photo is a pre-inspection routine for detail shops: four photos and a note at the car before the price is final, so a quoted three-hour job doesn't quietly turn into an unpaid full day. It's the same two moves as the video:

  1. Shoot the hood, the seats, the cargo area and the roof, and note anything like swirls, pet hair or sap.
  2. Flag any gap against the quote, so you revise before you start, not after.

And one rule: the condition call and the final price stay with the person at the car. Below is the checklist, a revision message to copy, how to set it up so it runs without anyone remembering to start it, and how to tell it's working.

Why the same package isn't the same job

A "full detail" on a well-kept two-year-old sedan and on a ten-year-old family hauler are different jobs with the same name. Heavy swirls, pet hair, embedded contamination, sap, a previous coating of unknown origin or a repainted panel can each turn a three-hour quote into a full day. The classic failure in this trade is the job that costs more than it was quoted, and it almost always starts with a price given before anyone looked closely at the car.

So the inspection is the stage that decides whether the job makes money. Our longer page on AI for auto detailing businesses covers the other stages.

Who it is for

  • One- and two-person detail shops where the person polishing is also quoting and answering the phone.
  • Mobile detailers who quote by text before they ever see the vehicle.
  • Shops that sell coatings or paint correction, where condition changes the whole job.

What you need first

  • A phone with a camera, and a place to attach photos to the job (your booking tool, a shared folder or a simple job sheet).
  • Your package list with the price range you quote for each vehicle size.
  • A rule for how big a gap triggers a revision (for example, more than an hour of extra work).
  • The customer's permission to message them a revised quote before work starts.

Step 1. Four photos and a note

At drop-off or on arrival, before any product touches the car, take the same four photos every time:

Photo What to look for Note it as
Hood Swirls, scratches, oxidation, previous coating "swirls", "oxidation", "coated?"
Seats Pet hair, stains, odors, worn leather "pet hair", "stains"
Cargo area Sand, spills, mats, hair "cargo heavy"
Roof Sap, bird droppings, water spots "sap", "spots"

Add a one-line note with anything else that changes the work: a repainted panel, a ceramic coating you didn't install, a part that's loose. Good photos also help later. Google asks that Business Profile photos be sharp and well lit and says the image should represent reality, which is exactly what an honest before shot is.

Step 2. Flag the gap before you start

Compare what you found with what was quoted. If the extra work passes your rule, stop and revise before starting. The flag should say three things: what was quoted, what you found, and what the job now is.

Scope changed: Job B, 2016 Chevy Suburban (demo). Quoted: full detail, about 3 hours. Found: heavy swirls on the hood, pet hair throughout, sap on the roof. Now: a full-day job.

The revision message to copy

Hi [first name], we've had a close look at your [vehicle] before starting. We found [the two or three biggest items], which takes the job from [quoted time] to [new time]. Here's the revised quote: [link or amount]. Reply YES to go ahead, or call me and we'll talk through options, like doing the interior today and the paint another time. [your name], [shop]

Offer an option, not only a higher number. A customer who can choose a smaller job is far less likely to feel cornered.

The rule: the person at the car decides

You'd think a smart tool should just send the new price itself. It shouldn't. Whether a finish can be safely corrected, whether a coating is worth it on that paint and what the job will really take are judgments made by a person looking at the vehicle. A tool can collect the photos, tag the notes, compare them with the quote and draft the message. A person approves the price and sends it.

Make it run without being asked

In a one- or two-person shop, anything that has to be triggered gets dropped by week three. So set it up to arrive prepared:

  1. Every booking creates its inspection checklist automatically, with the four photo slots empty.
  2. A job can't be marked started until the four photos are attached.
  3. When the notes include any word on your "scope" list (swirls, pet hair, sap, coated), a revision draft appears for approval.
  4. The approved revision is saved on the job, so the final invoice matches what the customer agreed to.

What to watch out for

  • Don't skip the photos on "easy" cars. The habit only works if it's every car.
  • Don't promise how long a coating will last. That depends on the product, the conditions and the customer's own care.
  • Don't let a draft go out unread. A revised price is a promise.
  • Keep your price ranges written down. The SBA's small business guide advises that a marketing plan should explain your pricing strategy and how you'll use promotions, and a written range by vehicle size and condition is the start of that.

How to tell it worked

For a month, track every job's quoted time against its real time, and how many got a revision before starting. The number of jobs that ran long without a revision should fall toward zero. Your before and after photos become your marketing, too, and reviews matter here: BrightLocal's 2026 survey found 97% of consumers read reviews for local businesses, and a customer who agreed to the real price up front is a happier reviewer. Keep your profile's services and hours current as well, since Google says complete and accurate information is more likely to show. Questions people ask

Why do full details take longer than quoted?

Because the same package name covers very different cars. Swirls, pet hair, sap or an old coating can turn a three-hour job into a full day, and that usually isn't known until someone inspects the car.

What photos should a detailer take before starting?

The same four every time: the hood, the seats, the cargo area and the roof, plus a short note on anything that changes the work, like a repainted panel or a previous coating.

Should software send a revised detailing quote automatically?

No. It can collect the photos, compare them with the quote and draft the message, but the person at the car judges the condition and approves the final price.

How do I make inspections stick in a small shop?

Make them automatic: every booking creates the checklist, a job can't start without the four photos, and scope words in the notes create a revision draft for approval.

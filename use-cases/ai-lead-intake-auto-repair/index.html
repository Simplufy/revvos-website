AI lead intake auto repair: capture every new inquiry in one record

AI lead intake auto repair shops use should catch every new inquiry (phone, form, text, Google message) in one record with six fields and a named owner, and let AI fill fields, flag urgency and draft a first reply. Price, safety and unhappy customers stay with a person.

Updated October 17, 2026 · 5 min read

AI lead intake auto repair shops can set up solves a narrow problem: a new customer reaches out, and what they said gets lost before anyone acts on it. The inquiry might come by phone, a website form, a text, a Google Business Profile message or a social DM. Intake is the job of catching all of them in one place, with the same few fields filled in, and putting each in front of a named person fast.

It is not booking and it is not follow-up on an estimate. Those have their own pages: AI phone answering and estimate follow up. This page is about the first ten minutes of a new inquiry.

Where inquiries leak

Most shops have four or five inbound doors and one person watching all of them between customers. Three leaks are common:

  • After hours. Podium's Brett Steele told Repairer Driven News that 36% of collision repair customers try to contact a shop outside regular hours, and cited CCC data that 37.8% of body shop searchers search outside normal hours. The article is from 2019 and the speaker is a messaging vendor, so it is directional. If your own forms and voicemail show a similar pattern, it matters.
  • Unknown numbers. Hiya's 2026 report says 86% of calls from unknown numbers go unanswered. A new customer is, by definition, an unknown number, and may not leave a voicemail.
  • Copy and paste. A web form emails the owner, who forwards it to the advisor, who reads it two days later. Each handoff is a place for it to die.

The fields to capture

Every inquiry, from any source, should end up with the same record. Keep it short:

  1. Name and a callback number (confirmed, not assumed)
  2. Vehicle: year, make, model, and the VIN if they have it handy
  3. What they want in their own words (copy it exactly)
  4. How urgent: undriveable, warning light, routine
  5. Source: which door they came through
  6. Time received, and who owns it now

Resist adding twelve more. A long form drops the customer. The goal is that the advisor can make the call with everything they need and no re-asking.

What AI should and should not do here

Reasonable for AI: - Read an inbound message or transcript and fill the fields above - Flag urgency words ("won't start," "brakes grinding," "tow") - Spot duplicates, such as the same number calling and then submitting the form - Draft a first reply for a person to review - Show the source and say plainly when a field is missing

Leave to a person: - Anything about price before the car is inspected - Whether a vehicle is safe to drive - Declining a customer, such as for out-of-scope work, with the reason - The first real conversation, if the customer is upset

Speed, and who owns it

Set a clock. Something like: any inquiry gets a human reply within one business hour, and anything marked urgent within fifteen minutes. These numbers are examples, not benchmarks; choose what your front desk can hold. What matters is that every inquiry has a named owner and a visible time.

Review the board once a day. Which inquiries have no owner? Which are older than your clock? A simple list sorted by age does more than a smart tool nobody opens.

Your shop system still holds the truth

An intake record is not the repair order. When someone confirms the appointment, the customer and vehicle should land in your shop-management system without retyping. Tekmetric lists 70+ integrations and offers API access by application; Shopmonkey and AutoLeap have their own front-desk AI add-ons. Ask any intake tool how it writes back, and what a person has to copy over by hand.

Consent and texting

If your intake replies by text from software, the sending number has to be registered, per Twilio's A2P 10DLC documentation. A customer who texted you first and asked a question is different from a marketing list, but have your lawyer confirm what applies in your state. If someone says stop, stop.

What to measure

Count weekly: inquiries by source, how many had a reply inside your clock, how many became appointments, and how many had missing fields. Skip vendor lead-conversion promises. You will have your own baseline in a month.

Questions people ask

What is AI lead intake for an auto repair shop?

It collects new customer inquiries from calls, web forms, texts and messages into one record with the same fields (contact, vehicle, request, urgency, source, owner), so none are lost between doors.

What information should a shop capture from a new lead?

Name and confirmed callback number, vehicle year, make and model, what they want in their own words, urgency, source and time received. Keep it short so customers do not drop off.

How fast should a shop reply to a new inquiry?

Set a clock you can hold, such as one business hour and fifteen minutes for urgent. Those are examples, not benchmarks. Every inquiry needs a named owner.

Do people contact auto shops after hours?

Podium's Brett Steele said 36% of collision repair customers try to contact a shop outside regular hours, in a 2019 Repairer Driven News article. Check your own form and voicemail times.

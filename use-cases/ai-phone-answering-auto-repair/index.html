AI phone answering auto repair: a one-week trial plan

AI phone answering auto repair shops buy should be tested for one normal week before signing: log every call, count booked appointments, clean handoffs, customer-visible mistakes and dead-end calls, and decide beforehand what it may never do alone, such as quoting uninspected work.

Updated October 17, 2026 · 5 min read

AI phone answering auto repair shops can buy today is easy to demo and hard to judge. Every vendor plays you a smooth call. What matters is what happens on the ugly ones: the caller with a noisy engine and no vehicle info, the one who wants "Mike," the one who is already angry. This page is a one-week trial plan you can run before you sign anything.

Our other pages cover what an AI phone agent does and how to cut missed calls. This one is about proving it works in your shop.

Why shops look at this at all

Calls go unanswered because the people who would answer them are busy with customers. Hiya's 2026 report says 86% of calls from unknown numbers go unanswered, and that is people picking up their own phones, not shops. For repair, CDK reports that 61% of customers still call to schedule service. That figure comes from a dealership vendor, so read it as a hint about habits, not a count of your callers.

Shop software vendors have noticed. AutoLeap's AIR books into the AutoLeap calendar and lists per-call and monthly plans. Shopmonkey's Concierge takes calls after hours, captures the customer and vehicle, and can transfer or create follow-up tasks. If your shop already runs one of those systems, ask them first.

The one-week trial

Pick a week that has a normal mix of work. Do not run it the week of a holiday.

Before the week (an hour):

  1. Write down the five calls you hear most: appointment request, status of my car, price on a job, "is it done," and a parts or vendor call.
  2. Write the three things the system must never do on its own: quote a price for work you have not inspected, promise a pickup time, or tell a customer a repair is safe to drive on.
  3. Decide who gets the transfer, and a backup. A transfer to nobody is worse than a ring.

During the week:

  • Keep a plain log, one line per call: time, what the caller wanted, what the system did, whether a person had to fix it.
  • Call it yourself six or seven times. Use a bad cell connection. Mumble the make. Ask for a person by name. Ask something it should not answer.
  • Have your front-desk person mark every call where they thought "I would have said that differently."

At the end of the week, count four things:

  • Calls that ended with a booked appointment that is really on the calendar
  • Calls it handed to a person, and how many of those handoffs came with the right information
  • Calls it got wrong in a way a customer would notice
  • Calls where it did nothing useful and the caller hung up

If you cannot get those four numbers from the vendor's own dashboard, that tells you something about the vendor.

What to ask on the demo call

  • Where does the booking land? In your shop-management system, or in a separate calendar someone has to copy over? Tekmetric lists 70+ integrations and gives API access by application, so ask the specific system, not "do you integrate."
  • What happens when it does not know? The right answer is a handoff with the notes captured so far.
  • Can you hear the recordings and read transcripts? Insist on both.
  • What does it cost per call and per month, and what counts as a call? AutoLeap, for example, publishes its tiers. Treat a vendor who will not give a number as a red flag.
  • Who owns the phone number? You should be able to leave and keep it.

Rules to check before go-live

Recording a call can require consent from everyone on it. The Digital Media Law Project lists states such as California, Florida, Illinois and Pennsylvania as all-party consent states, so the greeting should say the call may be recorded if you record. Your lawyer should confirm your state.

On inbound calls, the FCC has said the TCPA's requirements do not extend to technologies used to answer inbound calls. Outbound is different: the FCC ruled in 2024 that AI-generated voices count as "artificial" under the TCPA. If the same vendor also calls your customers back, ask how they handle consent.

What we would not hand over

  • Price quotes on work nobody has inspected
  • Anything about whether a car is safe to drive
  • Handling a complaint. A person should call that customer back the same day.

Questions people ask

How do I test an AI phone answering system for my shop?

Run it for one normal week with a one-line log per call. Count booked appointments that really landed on the calendar, handoffs with the right information, customer-visible mistakes and calls that went nowhere. Also call it yourself with a bad connection and awkward questions.

Does AI phone answering work with Tekmetric or Shopmonkey?

It depends on the product. Tekmetric lists 70+ integrations and gives API access by application, and Shopmonkey and AutoLeap sell their own phone assistants. Ask which system the booking lands in and whether a person has to copy it over.

Do I have to tell callers the call is recorded?

Some states require consent from everyone on the call. The Digital Media Law Project lists states such as California, Florida, Illinois and Pennsylvania. Put a recording notice in the greeting and confirm your state with a lawyer.

Do the TCPA rules apply to AI answering inbound calls?

The FCC has said TCPA requirements do not extend to technologies used to answer inbound calls. Outbound calls with AI voices are different, since the FCC treats AI-generated voices as artificial under the TCPA.

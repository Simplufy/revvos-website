The True Stage Rule: repair status text templates that stay accurate

These repair status text templates follow the true stage rule: first agree who changes each stage on the repair order and when, then text on a few stages (checked in, estimate ready, waiting on parts, ready), and have a person call with bad news such as a part delayed again.

Updated October 21, 2026 · 6 min read

The true stage rule is three steps, in the same order as the video:

  1. Make the stage true. Agree who changes each stage on the repair order and when, so "ready" never means "almost."
  2. Text on a few stages. Checked in, estimate ready, waiting on parts and ready for pickup cover most of what customers want to know.
  3. Call with bad news. A part delayed again, a bigger repair than quoted or a car that won't be done today gets a call from a person.

Below are the stage definitions to agree on, a text for each stage, and the checks that stop a wrong stage from sending a wrong message.

Who this is for

Service managers and advisors whose phones ring all day with "is it done yet?" calls, at shops whose shop system or texting tool can send a message when a repair order changes stage. The texting part is usually easy. Christian Brothers Automotive, a franchise group, told Repairer Driven News in 2019 that most customers say yes when asked about text notifications, and that its automated updates are generic but give customers peace of mind. Its shops still phone to confirm the full scope of work before starting.

The hard part is the stage itself.

Why the stage comes first

Automation repeats whatever the repair order says. If a car is finished but the order still reads "in progress," the customer gets a wrong text, and then calls. AutoVitals, a shop-software vendor, wrote in March 2026 that unclear status causes limbo, duplicated work and late customer updates, and it recommends a clear status on every repair order with markers such as waiting for approval, parts on order and ready for pickup.

So the first job is a short meeting, not a setting.

Step one, make the stage true

Sit down with your advisors and techs for twenty minutes and agree on three things. Write the answers on one page and post it at the dispatch desk.

Who changes each stage, and when. Use a table like this one and fill in your own names.

Stage Means Changed by When
Checked in We have the car, the keys and the concern Advisor At drop-off
Inspection done Findings are written, estimate is being built Tech When the inspection is finished
Estimate ready The estimate is sent and waiting on the customer Advisor When it's sent
Waiting on parts Approved, a part isn't here yet Advisor When the part is ordered
In repair Work has started Tech When the car goes on the lift
Ready Work done, final check done, keys at the desk Advisor After the final check

What each stage means in plain words. "Ready" means the keys are at the desk and the invoice is done. It never means "almost."

What happens when a stage reverses. A car that goes back on the lift after a road test moves back to "In repair," and the shop system should not send a second "ready" text when it comes off again without a person checking.

Step two, text on a few stages

Not every stage change needs a message. Four cover most of what people want, and the Christian Brothers executive quoted above said their shops send about four notifications a day, which gives a sense of the ceiling. If a customer hears about every minor change, they stop reading.

Checked in

Hi [first name], it's [shop]. We have your [vehicle] and we'll text you when the inspection is done, usually by [time]. Reply here with any questions.

Estimate ready

Hi [first name], the estimate for your [vehicle] is ready. You can review it here: [link]. [Advisor] can walk you through it if you'd like a call.

Waiting on parts

Hi [first name], the part for your [vehicle] is on order. Our current date is [date]. We'll update you if that changes.

If you don't have a date, say so: "We don't have a delivery date yet. We'll text you as soon as we do."

Ready for pickup

Hi [first name], your [vehicle] is ready. We're open until [time], and the total is [amount]. Park in [spot] and come to the front desk.

Optional: an "in repair" message. Skip it if it only adds noise.

Step three, call with bad news

Keep a person on anything with weight. These get a phone call from the advisor, and a text can follow to confirm what was said:

  • A part delayed again, or delayed with no new date.
  • A bigger repair than quoted, or a new safety finding.
  • A car that won't be done today when the customer expected it.
  • Any reply like "why is it so much?" or "this is the second time."

Approvals stay with a person too. An estimate with a large number or a safety item needs a conversation, not a second template.

Collision shops

Collision repairs take longer, so updates matter more. CCC's UpdatePlus product sends automated text and email updates on repair status and a one-question survey on delivery day. The same rule applies: the update is only as right as the stage in the estimating or shop system.

Texting rules

Texting from an application over a standard 10-digit number in the US requires registration. Twilio's documentation describes A2P 10DLC as the carrier standard for verified, consensual application texting. Your texting vendor usually handles it, but ask. Get consent on the work order, honor a stop request on every channel, and have your own lawyer confirm what consent you need. This is general information, not legal advice.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Turning on texts before the stages are agreed. You automate the confusion.
  • A text for every click. Customers stop reading.
  • Templates for bad news. A delayed part needs a person.
  • "Ready" that means "almost." One wrong ready text costs more trust than ten right ones earn.

How to tell it worked

Count first. For two weeks before you switch anything on, tally the status calls your advisors take each day and how many were "is my car ready." Turn the four texts on, then count again for two weeks. Also count wrong texts, any message that went out on a stage that wasn't true. That number should be zero, and if it isn't, go back to step one. We can't give you a typical reduction in calls, because we have no sourced figure, and we'd doubt any vendor that quotes one without saying how it counted.

Questions people ask

Which repair stages should send the customer a text?

Checked in, estimate ready, waiting on parts and ready for pickup cover most of what customers want to know. An in-repair message is optional.

Why do status texts sometimes make customers call more?

Automation repeats whatever the repair order says. If the stage is wrong, the customer gets a wrong text and then calls, so agree on who changes each stage and when before turning texts on.

Should a delayed part be a text or a call?

A call from the advisor. A text can follow to confirm the new date, or to say plainly that there isn't one yet.

Do I need to register to send status texts from software?

In the US, application texting over a 10-digit number needs A2P 10DLC registration, which your texting vendor usually handles. Get consent on the work order and honor stop requests.

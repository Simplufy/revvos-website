Guide

The cost of AI for an auto repair shop is a structure, not a sticker price.

Most pages answer this question with a single number. That number is wrong for your shop, because the cost depends on your size, your channels, and how many systems have to talk to each other. What we can give you honestly is the structure: the layers that make up the real cost, how each one scales, and which line vendors leave off the quote.

The Cost Layers

Five lines, not one.

Every automation product in a shop, whatever it is, has the same five layers under the headline price. Read the quote against this list and the surprises mostly disappear.

Layer What drives it How it scales
The subscription Per shop, per seat, or per location, depending on the vendor's model. A per-shop price stays flat as you add staff. A per-seat price climbs with every advisor you add.
Usage Per-minute calls, per-message SMS, per-contact email, per-query model use. Scales with volume, so a busy shop with a full phone pays more than a quiet one on the same plan.
Implementation Setup, data mapping, connecting to your shop system, configuring the rules. A one-time cost that grows with how many systems have to be connected.
Maintenance Keeping the connection alive as your shop system and its API change. Ongoing, and larger when the integration is custom rather than supported.
Your staff time Reviewing prepared actions and approving them. This is a real cost, even when it is not on an invoice. Scales with how much the tool prepares and how many people touch the approvals.

When you are quoted a single monthly figure, ask which of these five it covers. A low subscription with uncapped usage is not a low cost, and a high subscription that includes setup and maintenance is not a high one.

How It Scales

One shop and an MSO buy the same thing differently.

A single shop usually cares about the subscription and the usage line, because staff time is the owner's own and does not feel like a cost. A multi-location operator cares about all five, because every location multiplies the subscription, the setup repeats, and the approvals land on people whose time is already spoken for. That is why a price that looks reasonable for one shop can be misleading when you multiply it across ten, and why an MSO should ask for per-location math before signing anything.

There is also a cost that appears only when the fit is wrong: churn. Ripping out an automation you adopted too early means paying the implementation twice, plus the disruption in between. That is the argument for starting with the smallest useful scope and expanding, rather than buying the whole stack on day one.

The Honest Part

We are not going to put a number on this page.

RevvOS pricing depends on the scope of what you connect and how many locations you run, and we do not publish a figure here, because any number we wrote would be wrong for most of the shops reading it. What we will do instead is scope it with you: which systems you connect, what the layer watches, and how many people approve its work. That conversation produces a real number, and it produces it from your shop rather than from a marketing page.

If you want to sanity-check any quote you receive, including ours, take it back to the five layers above and ask which are covered. That test is free, and it works on every vendor in this category.

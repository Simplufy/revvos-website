Alternative guide

Is your problem that you cannot get paid fast enough, or that your software does not fit?

Those two sentences sound similar at a counter and they are completely different problems. One is a cash-flow problem and the money to fix it usually comes from a finance product. The other is a software problem and the money to fix it usually comes from a migration. Choosing the wrong one is how a glass shop spends a year and a budget solving neither.

Map which one you have -> All integrations

Start here

Read down both columns. One of them will sound like your week.

Be honest about which column has more lines you recognize. Shops frequently have both problems at once, and in that case the cash problem is almost always the urgent one and the software problem is the expensive one.

Problem A

You cannot get paid fast enough

  • The glass is in and correct and money still has not arrived
  • Payroll and parts are due before the claim settles
  • You are turning down work because you cannot float the parts
  • Nobody can tell you which jobs are actually waiting on money
  • Insurance payouts and customer payments both sit unresolved
  • The receivables list is long and the ages are old
Problem B

Your software does not fit

  • The team works around the system instead of in it
  • Dispatch and routing do not match how your vans actually run
  • Two locations cannot see each other's jobs
  • Reporting requires someone to build a spreadsheet by hand
  • A vendor just raised the price and nothing got better
  • You have stopped asking for features because the answer is always no

Notice that none of the lines in column A are fixed by better software, and none of the lines in column B are fixed by faster money. That is the entire point of separating them before you shop.

If it is problem A

A cash gap is a timing problem, and timing has a shape.

In glass, the work finishes long before the money does. Seeing where your money actually sits on that timeline is what tells you which fix applies, because different fixes act on different parts of it.

DAY 0

Job complete

Glass installed, calibration done if needed, customer drives away. From here the shop has spent the money and has not been paid.

DAYS 1 TO 3

Claim submitted

This is where documentation failures cost the most. A missing photo or a mismatched part does not delay payment by a day, it returns the claim to the back of a queue.

THE GAP

Adjudicating

The job is done and the money is neither owed nor received. Every cash-flow product sold into glass exists to act on this window. Everything else on the timeline is bookkeeping.

WEEK 2 AND LATER

Payment received

Reconciled against the job. If this step is manual, the shop is behind on knowing what it has actually collected.

What actually addresses a cash-flow gap

A finance product that advances money against unpaid jobs

GlassBiller, as one example, describes providing advances for shops waiting on insurance payouts or customer payments, specifically to unlock cash flow. That is a finance answer to a finance problem. Whether it is available to you, on what terms, and at what cost is a question for the provider, and you should treat the cost of the advance as part of the comparison.

A receivables process, which is different from a receivables report

Every platform shows you an aging report. Far fewer make somebody work it. The fix is assigning the oldest unpaid jobs to a named person with a cadence, so the report becomes a queue rather than a warning light.

Cleaner documentation at submission

The cheapest cash-flow improvement available to most shops, because it prevents the resubmission cycle rather than financing it. This is the one place where an operating layer helps a cash problem, by catching the missing evidence before the claim goes out rather than after it comes back.

Advance and receivables features described above reflect the provider's own published positioning. Confirm terms, eligibility, and current capabilities directly with the provider.

What does not address it: switching to a different management system. A new platform will not make a payer settle faster, and the migration will consume cash at exactly the moment cash is tight.

If it is problem B

"The software does not fit" has two very different causes.

Before pricing a migration, work out which of these you are actually describing. The first justifies switching. The second usually does not, and shops switch anyway because it is the only lever their vendor relationship gave them.

CAUSE 1

The record system is wrong for glass

If the platform was built for mechanical repair and glass is an afterthought, the mismatch is structural and it will not be patched. Look for parts that cannot be matched by VIN, dispatch that assumes the vehicle comes to you, and no concept of a calibration, a mobile van, or an insurance claim attached to a job.

Answer: this is the case where a purpose-built glass platform genuinely is the alternative. GlassBiller describes itself as built for auto glass shops, with VIN matched parts, dispatch, and real-time job, technician, vehicle, and route tracking. Confirm the current feature set with the vendor against your own workflow before treating that as settled.

CAUSE 2

The record system is fine; the handoffs are not

The jobs are in the system. The photos are in the system. Nothing is being dropped because a field is missing. Things are being dropped because an approval came back and nobody acted on it, a documentation request sat unanswered, or a customer was never told their install moved. That is not a software deficiency and no migration fixes it.

Answer: an operating layer above the current platform. It watches the open items across jobs and claims, and prepares the next step for a person to approve, without the shop changing its system of record.

Two things shops switch for that often turn out not to need switching

Accounting sync. GlassBiller describes syncing with QuickBooks so that invoices and payments are automatically updated. If the real problem is that your books lag your jobs, the question to ask is whether your current platform can do the same, not whether to change platforms.

Late paperwork. If jobs are documented late or incompletely, that is a discipline problem with a software symptom. Confirm the current platform actually lacks the field before paying for a migration to get it.

The third option

A layer that sits above whichever system you keep.

The layer option is not a compromise between the two tracks. It answers a specific question: which open items at this shop have nobody assigned to them right now. It reads from the platform the shop already runs, identifies the stalled approval and the unanswered documentation request and the customer who has not been updated, and prepares the next step for a person to review.

It is worth being precise about what it is not. RevvOS is not a payment processor and does not advance money against unpaid jobs, so it does not solve problem A on its own. It is not a system of record, so it does not replace GlassBiller or any other platform your networks require. And it cannot change what an insurer will pay or how long adjudication takes.

It helps problem A by preventing resubmissions

Catching missing evidence before submission is the one cash-flow lever that is not a finance product.

It helps problem B when the cause is handoffs

Which is more often the case at a shop than a genuinely wrong platform.

It does nothing for a structural mismatch

If the platform cannot match parts by VIN, the answer is a different platform and we will tell you that.

See how the layer works alongside this platform on the GlassBiller integration page, or the equivalent for Omega EDI shops, where the constraint is the claim channel rather than the money.
